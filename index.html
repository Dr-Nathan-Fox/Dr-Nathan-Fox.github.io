Nathan Fox

AI Scientist, Michigan Institute for Data & AI in Society (MIDAS), University of Michigan

I work on AI education, research communication, and community building across the University of Michigan campus. My day-to-day work includes developing tutorials, planning events, and helping researchers engage with AI tools and resources. I am based in Michigan.

My research uses social media, crowdsourced data, and machine learning to understand how people experience and value landscapes, biodiversity, and geodiversity. Recent work includes computer vision for species detection from social media photographs and a proposal for valuing geosystem services alongside ecosystem services. I also develop open-source R packages for harvesting and analyzing public data.

Contact: foxnat@umich.edu

Current focus

I am interested in holistic approaches to monitoring land-use change under climate change. Land use shapes ecosystems, landscapes, and the places people value, but conventional monitoring relies on sparse survey data and slow reporting cycles that miss how people actually experience and change the land. I am working on frameworks that bring together three complementary sources of evidence: citizen-generated observations from social media, participatory GIS (PPGIS) that captures how residents and stakeholders value and map places, and extended reality (XR) that lets people explore and evaluate alternative futures in immersive, spatially accurate settings. Combining these streams with AI-assisted analysis can show not only where land use is changing, but how communities perceive those changes and what they want the landscape to become.
